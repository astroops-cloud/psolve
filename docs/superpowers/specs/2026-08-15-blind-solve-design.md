# Blind Solve — Design

**Date:** 2026-08-15
**Status:** proposed
**Size:** a milestone, comparable to M2 (the whole solver). Not a feature.
**Depends on:** `2026-08-15-stratified-selection-design.md` landing first

## 1. The problem

psolve cannot solve a frame without a pointing hint. It returns `NO_HINT` and
refuses. ASTAP can sweep the whole sky and does so routinely — AstroOps'
production invocation is `-r 180`, i.e. blind, with a hinted narrow-radius form
as the *retry*.

Measured on this machine:

```
ASTAP blind, frame that solves : ~0.10 s
ASTAP blind, frame that fails  :  5.42 s   (sweeps 3d,4d,6d ... 179d)
psolve, no hint                :  refuses
```

So psolve is **not a drop-in replacement**. Any frame with missing or sentinel
pointing — and `DEC = -90.` is used as "unset" in this archive — is solvable by
ASTAP and unsolvable by psolve.

## 2. Why the obvious approach does not work

The cheap idea is to reuse the existing hinted solver at many trial positions.
Costed from measured stage timings (extraction reused; only catalogue fetch,
catalogue quad build and match re-run, ~15.0 ms per trial):

| trial spacing | positions (dec <= +45) | time |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 deg | 35,189 | **529 s** |
| 1.5 deg | 15,639 | 235 s |
| 2.0 deg | 8,797 | 132 s |

Against ASTAP's 0.10-5.42 s, a positional sweep is 25-5000x worse. **Blind
solving needs a different data structure, not a faster loop.** Anything that
keeps the "try a position, fetch a disc, build quads" shape is dead on arrival.

## 3. Approach

Invert the search, as astrometry.net does.

Today: fetch catalogue in a disc -> build catalogue quads -> match against image
quads. The disc requires knowing where to look.

Blind: **precompute quad hashes for the whole catalogue at index-build time**
into a code-space search structure. At solve time, take each image quad's
4-vector code and look it up directly. Position falls out of the match rather
than being required as input.

psolve already has the hard half: `quad.rs::quad_code` produces a geometric
4-vector invariant under translation, rotation and scale, and `verify.rs` does
reprojection verification. What is missing is the precomputed side and the
search structure.

## 4. Scale bands — the reason this is a milestone

A quad code is scale-invariant, but *which four stars form a quad* is not. A
quad spanning 0.2 deg and one spanning 4 deg are different objects drawn from
different star populations. A blind index must therefore cover a range of
angular scales, because the frame's field size is unknown.

astrometry.net ships separate index files per scale band. psolve needs the
same: a set of bands, each holding quads whose diagonal falls in that band,
generated by tiling the sky at that band's scale.

Rough sizing, doubling bands from 0.25 deg to 8 deg, ~20 quads per tile:

```
band     tiles (dec<=+45)   quads
0.25       563,000        11.3 M
0.5        141,000         2.8 M
1.0         35,000         0.70 M
2.0          8,800         0.18 M
4.0          2,200         0.04 M
8.0            550         0.01 M
                          -------
                          ~15 M quads  x ~24 bytes  =  ~360 MB
```

Tractable — comparable to the existing G<=16 star index at 1.1 GB. The small
bands dominate, and they are also the ones that need the deepest stars: a
0.25 deg field at G<=14 may hold too few catalogue stars to form quads at all.
**Determining the shallowest usable band for this catalogue depth is a spike,
not an assumption.**

## 4a. Spike results — two assumptions in this spec are wrong

The §8 spike ran on 2026-08-15 against all three catalogue depths, read-only.
Full write-up: `/tmp/psolve-blind-spike.md`. It corrected two things below, and
validated a third.

**Build the blind index from G≤16, not G≤14.** Definition of usable: the
area-weighted sky fraction with fewer than 10 quads per tile stays under ~5%,
targeting 10-30 quads/tile.

| depth | shallowest usable band | note |
|---|---|---|
| G≤14 | **0.5°** | 0.25° fails on ~20% of sky by area — beyond galactic latitude ~40-60° a tile averages 4-10 stars, and 33-75% of tiles hold under 10 quads. Some hold none |
| G≤16 | **0.25°** | clears everywhere; worst case (galactic pole) still 25/25 quads |
| G≤18 | **0.25°** | ample, and unnecessary for this |

So §4's sizing table, which assumed G≤14, understates the required depth.
Blind solving at G≤14 would carry a **minimum field size** of about 0.5°.

**A uniform grid hash is not sufficient.** §5.2 guessed "the grid hash is simpler
and probably sufficient given the codes are bounded". Measured over 1.73M sampled
codes (G≤16, 1.0° band), the code space is **clustered, not uniform**:
coefficient of variation 0.74-1.51 across coordinate-pair histograms, hotspot
bins 3-9.3× the mean, and up to **39% of a naive equal-width grid empty**. A
quantile / equal-population grid or a kd-tree is required. Prototype before the
format is fixed.

**A per-tile emission cap is mandatory, not an optimisation.** Availability is
not the constraint — at the 1.0° band, G≤16 yields a median of **11,183**
formable quads per tile against a 10-30 target, two to three orders of magnitude
more than needed. Uncapped, total storage runs to 36-45M quads and 860 MB-1.08 GB.
With a 12-star budget and a 25-per-tile cap at 24 B/quad:

| depth | quads | size |
|---|---|---|
| G≤14 | 15.64 M | 375 MB |
| G≤16 | **18.67 M** | **448 MB** |
| G≤18 | 18.68 M | 448 MB |

That validates §4's ~15M / ~360 MB estimate, given the depth correction.

**Build cost is not a blocker.** A single-threaded sweep of all 750,998 tiles
across six bands is ~692 s (G≤14) to ~884 s (G≤18) — 12-15 minutes, dominated by
the 563k tiles in the 0.25° band, and embarrassingly parallel.

## 5. Components

1. **Quad generation at build time.** Reuse `quad.rs`'s combination logic, run
   per sky tile per band rather than per frame. Must be deterministic.
2. **A code-space search structure.** Given an image quad's 4-vector, find
   catalogue quads with nearby codes. A kd-tree over 4 dimensions, or a
   uniform grid hash on the code space. The grid hash is simpler and probably
   sufficient given the codes are bounded; prefer it unless measurement says
   otherwise.
3. **A new index artefact.** The existing `.psidx` is a star index and stays
   exactly as it is. This is a second file — call it `.psqidx` — with its own
   header, digest and version. **Do not extend `.psidx`'s format**; the star
   index is load-bearing for the working hinted path and must not churn.
4. **Candidate verification.** Blind matching produces false positives a hinted
   search cannot, because the null hypothesis is enormously larger. The
   existing Poisson log-odds gate was calibrated against a disc of known
   position; it must be re-derived for an all-sky null or it will pass
   coincidences. **This is the correctness crux of the milestone**, not the
   search structure.
5. **CLI.** `psolve solve <FILE> --index <star.psidx> --quad-index <q.psqidx>`
   with no `--hint`. In ASTAP mode, `-r 180` with no `-ra`/`-spd` selects it,
   which is what AstroOps already sends.

## 6. Acceptance criteria

1. **Solves without a hint** on frames whose hint is currently required, landing
   within 30" of the hinted answer.
2. **Beats a positional sweep decisively** — target under 5 s, i.e. comparable
   to ASTAP's failing case and within 50x of its solving case. Slower than the
   sweep is an automatic failure.
3. **Zero false positives on a null test.** Solve frames against a quad index
   built from a *different* part of the sky; every one must fail. A blind
   solver that confidently returns a wrong position is far worse than one that
   refuses, and this project has been bitten twice by measures that erred in
   the flattering direction.
4. **The hinted path is unchanged** — same solve rate, same separations, same
   timings on the 9,495-frame corpus. Blind is additive.
5. **Frames with sentinel pointing (`DEC = -90.`) now solve**, since those are
   the concrete cases motivating this.

## 7. Risks

**The verification gate is the hard part.** Everything else is engineering; the
false-positive question is science. Budget for it accordingly and do not treat
a working search as a working solver.

**Small bands may be unsupportable at G<=14.** If a 0.25 deg band cannot form
enough quads, blind solving has a minimum field size. That is an acceptable
limitation but it must be measured and documented, not discovered by a user.

**Build time and storage grow with band count.** The G<=16 star index built in
10 s; quad generation across six bands is a much larger job and should be
measured before the format is fixed.

**Scope discipline.** This milestone should deliver blind solving and nothing
else. The temptation to also fix CFA binning, or rework confidence generally,
should be resisted.

## 8. Suggested phasing

1. **Spike:** how many quads per band are actually formable at G<=14, and what
   is the shallowest usable band? Answer before committing to a format.
2. Quad index format, builder, digest, `info`.
3. Search structure, benchmarked against the 5 s target.
4. Verification gate re-derived for an all-sky null, with the null test as its
   acceptance.
5. CLI wiring, ASTAP-mode `-r 180` dispatch, docs.

## 9. Alternative, recorded so it can be rejected knowingly

A **wide-radius fallback** — retry at 30 deg when a hinted solve fails — is days
rather than a milestone. But it handles "the hint is wrong", **not** "there is
no hint". It would not solve the sentinel-pointing frames that motivate this,
and it is not what ASTAP does. It is not a substitute and should not be
presented as one.
